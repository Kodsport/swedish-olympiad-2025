\problemname{Ego Boosting}
\noindent

After several years of grinding programming, Tommy has realized that he is not confident at all.

To fix this, Tommy would like to boost his ego!

Initially, Tommy has $E$ ego. 

There are $N$ ego-increasing-problems (EP for short), where solving any of the EP will increase Tommy's ego by 1.
Each EP has a difficulty rating $a_i$.

To guarantee that Tommy solves an EP, Tommy must have at least the same amount of ego as the difficulty of the EP. 
In other words, $E \geq a_i$ must be satisfied for Tommy to be able to solve the $i$'th EP.

%blah blah blah before taking on any problem

Tommy can start solving any problem. 
But after solving a problem, he can only choose to attempt the closest unsolved problem to the left, or the closest unsolved problem to the right.
Before attempting any problem, Tommy can eat an ego-cookie to increase his ego by 1 unit. 

What is the minimum amount of ego-cookies Tommy has to eat to be able to solve all EPs?

\section*{Input}
\noindent
The first line contains two integers, $N$ $(1 \leq N \leq 5 \cdot 10^5)$ and $E$ $(0 \leq E \leq 10^9)$.

The second line contains $N$ integers, $a_i$ $(1 \leq a_i \leq 10^9)$ for $i = 1,\dots,N$, where the difficulty of the $i$:th EP from left to right is $a_i$.

\section*{Output}
\noindent
Print an integer: the minimum amount of ego-cookies Tommy has to eat to be able to solve all EPs.

\section*{Scoring}
Your solution will be tested on a set of test groups, each worth a number of points. Each test group contains
a set of test cases. To get the points for a test group you need to solve all test cases in the test group.

\section*{Example of Sample}
If we solve the EPs in the order $a_4, a_5, a_3, a_2, a_1, a_6$,
then 
\begin{itemize}
 \item Before solving $a_4 = 2$, Tommy has to eat a cookie to increase his ego to 2. After solving the problem Tommy's ego is 3.
 \item $a_5 = 3$, which Tommy can solve since his ego is already 3. After solving the problem his ego will become 4.
 \item Before solving $a_3 = 7$, Tommy has to eat 3 cookies to incerase his ego to 7. After solving the problem his ego becomes 8.
 \item $a_2 = 4$, which Tommy can solve since his ego is 8. Now his ego is 9.
 \item $a_1 = 9$, which Tommy can solve. Now his ego is 10.
 \item $a_6 = 10$, which Tommy can solve. Now his ego is 11.
\end{itemize}

Tommy eats in total 4 cookies, which can be proven to be the smallest amount of cookies Tommy needs.
