\problemname{Ego Boosting}
\noindent

After several years of grinding programming, Tommy has realized that he is not confident at all.

To fix this, Tommy would like to boost his ego! 

Initially, Tommy has $E$ ego. 

There are $N$ ego-increasing-problems (EP for short), where solving any of the EP will increase Tommy's ego by 1.
Each EP has a label which is a unique integer from $1$ to $N$, and a difficulty rating $a_i$.

To guarantee that Tommy solves an EP, Tommy must have at least the same amount of ego as the difficulty of the EP. 
In other words, $E \geq a_i$ must be satisfied for Tommy to be able to solve the $i$'th EP.

The first EP that Tommy chooses to solve could be any of the $N$ problems.
But after solving an EP labeled $x$, he can only choose to attempt either
\begin{itemize}
  \item The unsolved EP with the largest label less than $x$, or
  \item The unsolved EP with the smallest label greater than $x$.
\end{itemize}

At any time during his ego-increasing journey, Tommy can eat an ego-cookie to increase his ego by 1 unit. 

What is the minimum number of ego-cookies Tommy has to eat to be able to solve all EPs?

\section*{Input}
\noindent
The first line contains two integers, $N$ $(1 \leq N \leq 5 \cdot 10^5)$ and $E$ $(0 \leq E \leq 10^9)$.

The second line contains $N$ integers, $a_i$ $(1 \leq a_i \leq 10^9)$ for $i = 1,\dots,N$, 
where the difficulty of the EP labeled $i$ is $a_i$.

\section*{Output}
\noindent
Print an integer: the minimum number of ego-cookies Tommy has to eat to be able to solve all EPs.

\section*{Scoring}
Your solution will be tested on a set of test groups, each worth a number of points. Each test group contains
a set of test cases. To get the points for a test group you need to solve all test cases in the test group.

\section*{Explanation of Samples}
If we solve the EPs in the order $a_4, a_5, a_3, a_2, a_1, a_6$, the process is as follows:  
\begin{itemize}
 \item Before solving $a_4 = 2$, Tommy eats 1 cookie to increase his ego to 2. After solving the EP, his ego becomes 3.
 \item For $a_5 = 3$, Tommy's ego is already 3, so he solves it without eating a cookie. His ego increases to 4.
 \item Before solving $a_3 = 7$, Tommy eats 3 cookies to raise his ego to 7. After solving the EP, his ego becomes 8.
 \item For $a_2 = 4$, Tommy's ego is 8 (no cookies needed). After solving it, his ego becomes 9.
 \item For $a_1 = 9$, Tommy solves it directly. His ego increases to 10.
 \item Finally, for $a_6 = 10$, Tommy solves it with his current ego of 10. His ego becomes 11.
\end{itemize}

In total, Tommy eats 4 cookies, which is the minimum number required to solve all EPs.
